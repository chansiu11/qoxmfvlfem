# General-mode tactical bots

Only the spike/general-mode adapter uses `spike-tactics.js`; deathmatch and battle royale continue calling their existing AI update.

Team plans alternate grouped entry, split entry through Mid, and an initial default-control pause. Attackers can rotate after recent team vision reports show multiple defenders near the planned site, with time and cooldown checks. Human spike carriers determine which site teammates support. Defenders distribute between A, B and Market; recent sightings can trigger support rotations. After planting, attackers hold approaches and defenders approach together briefly, with one designated defuser and other bots supporting. The wait is skipped near the defuse deadline. A nearby teammate's visible fight triggers trade support.

Combat alternates a short lateral reposition, settling, and stationary bursts. Reloading and low health trigger cover searches; footsteps use quiet movement on cautious approaches. The existing reaction delay, aim error, field of view, line of sight, friendly firing-lane check, pathfinding and weapon movement limits remain active. These are scripted adaptations of tactical play, not a model trained on player footage or a claim of human-level skill. No agent abilities are added.

References: Riot, VALORANT Patch Notes 3.0 (stopping before shooting and walking accuracy); Riot, Peeking into VALORANT's Netcode (movement accuracy and angle holding); Red Bull Campus Clutch map strategies and Ascent guide (Mid control, split attacks, rotations).

Tests cover expiring/team-private reports, evidence-based rotations, planting, one defuser, stationary firing, and actual pathfinding from spawn through a site approach to completed planting. Browser/device playtests are not represented by the Node simulation.

During the 30-second preparation phase, bots now choose and travel to staging positions based on the same round plan: attacking A/B approaches or Mid Top for split entry, defending A/B site entrances or Mid Bottom. Navigation checks the entire path against the team's preparation area, so shortcuts through neutral/enemy territory are rejected. Bots hold and watch the approach when they reach their target. Purchases are valid throughout the connected preparation side.

Preparation boundary correction: attackers stop at A Lobby's exit, B Lobby's exit, and Mid Top instead of being able to occupy the forward A/B Main approaches. Defender boundaries remain on the site/Tree/Mid Bottom entrances. `PREP_STAGING` is shared with the tactical AI so waiting positions track these limits rather than using separate hard-coded coordinates. These are boundaries for the existing hand-built 2D Ascent geometry, not verified Riot collision/barrier coordinates.

Individual tendencies: a deterministic per-identity profile persists between ticks and rounds. Profiles vary entry/support/flank/anchor preference, reaction timing, safe movement tempo, burst/reposition rhythm, spacing, starting delay, preferred engagement range, caution and quiet-approach distance. Intermediate route points and preparation waiting positions are offset only onto navigable space. The spike carrier retains the objective route, and urgent plant/defuse timing continues to override nonessential delays. Profiles do not increase maximum weapon movement speed or reveal enemies.

Adaptive navigation replaces the general-mode bot's old random stuck-recovery movement. It keeps a short route cache, detects lost forward progress in elapsed simulation time, replans around blocked corners, projects blocked goals onto walkable space, and can reconsider a blocked attack lane. The route search adds bounded teammate crowd and recent team-traffic costs; local steering adds personal space and turn continuity. Starting next to a wall uses the true collision radius before gradually adding route clearance, preventing corner-adjacent starts from being rejected entirely. Movement remains collision-checked and bounded by weapon speed. Tests reproduce a wall-corner stall and two bots sharing the same start/destination, alongside full preparation and planting simulations.

Angle reservations: preparation, defense, post-plant and retake support use stable team/context-specific lane assignments. Each site provides distinct approach/backline targets and walkable holding positions. Alive teammate reservations and nearby humans' actual facing directions influence assignment; deaths release reservations. With more bots than named lanes, extra bots use separate crossfire positions. Visible combat threats still take priority over passive angle assignments. Tests check five distinct A-site targets and positions, stable assignments during movement, walkable holding spots and replacement of a fallen teammate.

Preparation privacy: opponents' actual player/bot positions and spawn coordinates are concealed in viewer-specific preparation snapshots. Rendering hides opponents even during initial state setup, and footsteps/directional indicators plus other opponent spatial sounds are suppressed until the round begins. Phase changes clear stale footstep tracks/events. Bot firing now validates the current target entity, live position, facing cone and wall line of sight immediately before creating a shot, rather than relying only on a cached perception point.

Shared-angle cap: watch and combat requests now reserve the actual local aim sector as well as the named holding lane. At most two nearby teammates may watch the same entrance/sector. Overflow bots temporarily adopt rear guard or a legal lateral lurking goal, and their alternate watch sectors are capped as well. Objective carriers and designated defusers keep moving toward their objective while surplus aim directions are redirected. Expiring reservations and dead teammates release capacity. Tests cover 12 bots requesting one idle angle and six bots simultaneously spotting an enemy from the same position; only two receive the primary/combat aim.

### Active movement and spectator controls
- Entry preparation pauses are 0.9–2.3 seconds; initial stagger is at most 0.45 seconds. Quiet approaches begin nearer sites (380–680 units).
- Entry and flank personalities close excessive combat distance after 650 ms; retake regroup waits at most 900 ms.
- Non-anchor defenders periodically use collision-checked 80-unit lateral peeks or 150-unit forward positions. One anchor keeps its assigned position. Existing two-bot angle reservations and visible-target-only firing still apply.
- Spectator controls have explicit dimensions that survive saved UI positioning; left/right buttons, arrow keys and canvas mouse clicks cycle targets. Switching immediately updates the camera, filters concealed preparation snapshots and deduplicates participant IDs.
- Verified with packed-game VM regression tests; device/browser playtesting remains unperformed.

### More active bots (2026-10-15)
Initial stagger is at most 0.2 seconds and default entry pause is 0.4–1.2 seconds. Non-anchor defenders reconsider collision-checked peek positions every 1.6–2.4 seconds, using 110-unit lateral or 220-unit forward positions. Non-anchor combat roles close excessive distance after 450 ms, trade support reaches teammates within 650 units, and retake regroup lasts at most 450 ms. Lurkers travel at normal weapon speed; close site approaches still walk quietly. Removed redundant crowded-teammate waiting because navigation already handles spacing. Two-bot angle limits, line-of-sight firing and movement speed limits remain.

### Fast entry and defender footstep communication
Attackers enter at normal weapon speed without staging pauses or stopping to trade for nearby teammates. After an actual shot, a single random 0–1 second timer runs; on expiry they resume their site route for at least 1.5 seconds even if the target stays visible. This timer resets each round. Aim-sector overflow also yields to entry rather than stalling it.
Defenders share enemy footsteps only when one living defender can hear a fresh event inside its audible range. Reports expire after 4.5 seconds and nominate the nearest site; all idle defenders, including anchors, rotate there. Reports do not grant enemy vision or authorize shooting. Visible enemies take combat priority; low HP and being hit no longer trigger retreat for defenders, though empty magazines/reloading can still seek cover.

### Entry firing fix
Stand-ground engagements no longer halt outside weapon range: bots close to 92% of maximum range, then fire. When an ally blocks the firing lane, they choose a collision-checked lateral position instead of waiting indefinitely. Regression simulations use real perception and projectile creation, with fake elapsed time, for both out-of-range approach and a friendly blocker.

### First-seen target lock
General-mode bots retain their first acquired enemy while it remains alive, hostile, within vision range and field of view, and unobstructed by walls. A closer newcomer does not replace it. Lost visibility, death or disconnect clears both the lock and perception cache so a new visible enemy can be selected immediately. Targets are revalidated from current actor state each tick; the lock resets every round.
